Project 01 — CFD · Hydrodynamics · URANS

BB2 Submarine Sail
Unsteady Load Investigation

ANSYS Fluent URANS k-ω SST NACA 0022 Vortex Shedding Strouhal Analysis Mesh Convergence

Investigating the onset conditions for unsteady hydrodynamic loads on a BB2-class submarine sail using URANS CFD. The study establishes operating envelopes where periodic vortex shedding becomes structurally significant — directly relevant to manoeuvring stability and control surface authority at high angles of attack.

Methodology
01 / Validation
Cylinder Benchmark
Transient solver validated against canonical von Kármán vortex shedding on a 2D cylinder at Re ≈ 10⁴–10⁵. Strouhal number recovered within St = 0.21 ± 0.02, confirming solver fidelity before proceeding to complex geometry.
02 / Mesh
Convergence Study
Systematic refinement from 43,750 to 700,000 cells. y⁺ < 1 maintained with structured boundary-layer inflation. First layer height 6.28 × 10⁻⁶ m. PISO pressure-velocity coupling. 0.001 s timestep × 4,000 steps.
03 / Simulation
Transient URANS
k-ω SST turbulence model. AoA swept 0°–22°, velocity 6–24 m/s. Unsteady vortex shedding only emerged at 22° AoA and 24 m/s — periodic Cl and Cd fluctuations confirmed vortex street formation.

Why y⁺ < 1 matters

Maintaining y⁺ below 1 places the first computational cell within the viscous sublayer — the thin region immediately adjacent to the wall where velocity varies linearly with distance. This eliminates wall function approximations and allows direct resolution of shear stress, boundary layer growth, and the onset of flow separation. For unsteady simulations where separation timing is critical to vortex shedding prediction, this resolution level is non-negotiable.

Key Findings
01 / Finding
Vortex Onset Threshold
Unsteady loading only emerged at 22° AoA with 24 m/s freestream. Flows at moderate AoA (0°–18°) remained fully attached and steady across all tested Reynolds numbers — consistent with NACA 0022 stall characteristics.
02 / Finding
Mesh Discontinuity Effect
A visible interface discontinuity near the airfoil leading edge introduced spurious numerical oscillations in the boundary layer. Mesh reconstruction with smooth transitions resolved this and enabled physically accurate separation prediction.
03 / Finding
Computational Constraints
Limited to 4 CPU cores. Water properties were substituted with air due to floating-point divergence under high-density flow conditions. Future work targets BB2-representative Reynolds numbers with restored water properties.
04 / Finding
Strouhal Frequency Prediction
St = 0.1–0.25 for NACA 0022 in post-stall provides first-order prediction of vortex shedding frequency for structural fatigue assessment, enabling design margins and control-law tuning for high-AoA manoeuvring envelopes.
